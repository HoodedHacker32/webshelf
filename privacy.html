Privacy

Last updated 5 October 2026.

In short

  • Webshelf has no accounts, no ads, no analytics and no cookies.
  • Webshelf keeps no record of your searches. Its search server passes each search on and forgets it.
  • To find results and answers, your searches are sent to the search engines and open-data services listed below. Each of them sees your search and your IP address, as any website you visit would, and has its own privacy policy.
  • Your settings and (if you choose) your search history stay in your browser.

What stays in your browser

Webshelf saves these in your browser’s own storage, on this device only. They are never sent to Webshelf, and you can clear them at any time in Settings or by clearing this site’s data in your browser:

  • your settings (theme, results source, safe search, language and the rest);
  • your recent searches, if “Remember my searches on this device” is on;
  • a location for weather and time, if you set one.

Answers from Wikipedia, Wikidata and similar services are kept for up to half an hour in the browser tab’s session storage, so the same lookup isn’t repeated. It is cleared when the tab closes.

Where your searches go

Each service, what it receives and when:

GitHub Pages (hosts this site)
The pages you open, including the address of a results page, which contains your search. GitHub’s privacy statement applies.
Webshelf’s search server (on Render, in the US)
Every search, which it passes on to Bing and Mwmbl; for the image row and the Images tab also to Flickr, DeviantArt, ArtStation, Pinterest, Imgur, Pixabay, Pexels, Openverse, Wikimedia Commons and Bing Images; for the Videos tab to Bing Videos, YouTube, Dailymotion, PeerTube and Vimeo; plus share names and symbols for prices, which it passes on to Yahoo Finance. With suggestions on, also what you type, which it passes on to Bing. It keeps no record of searches; Render, its host, may keep standard request logs for a short time.
Image and video sites
Thumbnails load from the sites they belong to (Flickr, DeviantArt, Pinterest, Bing and the others above), which see your IP address but not your search.
Mwmbl
Every search, directly, while the search server is waking up.
Wikipedia and Wikidata
Every search, for the knowledge panel, answers, spelling and related searches. Also what you type, if suggestions are on and the search server is waking up.
Wikimedia Commons
Searches that aren’t answered by a tool, for photos in the image row and topic headers.
MusicBrainz
Short searches that aren’t on Wikipedia, to recognise bands and artists, and song searches for lyrics cards.
TVmaze
The IMDb numbers of TV shows in a topic’s carousel, for their posters.
Openverse, Dailymotion, SepiaSearch, the Internet Archive
Searches on the Images and Videos tabs.
Open Library
Searches on the Books tab. Book covers load from Open Library too.
CoinGecko
Searches for a cryptocurrency price.
Wiktionary
Words you look up with “define”.
Open-Meteo
Place names or your saved location, for weather and time searches.
Frankfurter
Currency codes, for currency conversions.
The websites in your results
Only when you click a result. Webshelf’s links don’t tell them which search you came from.

Your country, for questions like “who is the current president”, is worked out on your device from its time zone or your saved location. Nothing is looked up to find it.

Questions

Webshelf is open source, so everything above can be checked in its code. To ask about privacy or report a problem, open an issue on GitHub.
